A small, evidence-led experiment for selling deployment and infrastructure work. Direct software teams are the primary audience. Agencies qualify only when there is evidence they want an outside specialist.

Public operating outline, revised September 2026. An experiment—not a claim of validated demand. Individual prospects, contact details and outreach drafts stay separate.

What to sell

One bounded implementation: a release pipeline, repeatable deployment or operational workflow. Agree on deliverables, access, acceptance criteria, timeline and price before implementation. If scope is unknown, propose a separately agreed paid diagnostic.

Hand over configuration, verification evidence and a runbook. No 24/7 coverage, unlimited support, uptime guarantees or unverified compliance expertise.

Which signals matter

  1. A specific project or contractor request.

    The strongest starting point. Verify the posting is still open, the budget is viable, the scope fits, and the requested route permits a response.

  2. A relevant operational change.

    A company publicly describes a migration, launch or infrastructure need. A conversation may be relevant, but budget and interest in external help remain unknown.

  3. Funding or full-time hiring.

    Useful for finding companies, not proof they want this service. A paid interview trial is not a standalone client project. Never enter a hiring process while concealing a supplier pitch.

Ten working days

Days 1–2: prepare a short sandbox demonstration and confirm the inbox, contractual clearance and available delivery hours. Use only your own or permissioned material.

Days 3–8: spend 30 minutes a day finding and qualifying current requests. Record the source, observed date, actual problem, contractor status, contact route and disqualifiers. Respond to at most two well-matched opportunities per day after individual review. Do not fill a quota with bad fits.

Days 9–10: follow up once where appropriate, qualify interested replies and propose one bounded scope. Measure real conversations and scopes requested—not email opens or the size of the list.

The first message

Point to the actual request. State one relevant outcome you could deliver. Ask a question that establishes fit. Make your independent-contractor status clear and use the requested application or business channel.

For example: “I saw your request for a repeatable deployment workflow. I work on scoped infrastructure implementations with a documented handoff. Is a short implementation engagement an option, or do you need ongoing coverage?”

Template only. Tailor it to the real request; do not imply experience, availability or a relationship you have not established.

Before paying for leads

Ask for a current sample, dated source records, contact-verification dates, a clear definition of buying intent and written cancellation terms. Funding is not buying intent. A working checkout is not proof the service is still being maintained.

Use a small, manually reviewed batch before buying subscriptions, databases or ads. No purchases, signups or outreach are authorized by this plan.

Qualification before volume

Reject closed postings, full-time roles disguised as consulting opportunities, unclear identities, implausible budgets and mandatory skills or location requirements you cannot meet. Agencies are not automatically competitors or partners: explicit outsourcing demand is what changes their relevance.

Keep communication on the platform when required. Do not guess private email addresses, use support tickets as sales channels, or share credentials in an initial brief. Honor opt-outs and applicable outreach rules.
