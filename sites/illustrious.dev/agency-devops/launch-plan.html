One offer. One paid project. Then improve it.

A two-week launch experiment for project-based deployment and infrastructure help for software agencies.

Prepared 7 September 2026. This is a proposed operating plan, not a claim of customers, revenue or validated demand. This page is public; prospect research and individual outreach drafts are kept separately.

Sell the engineering outcome, not the tool.

Help an agency complete one CI/CD, container deployment or operational-automation project. Define the scope, quote the work, verify the result and leave a usable handoff. Refine delivery automation behind that service.

What we are testing

Whether a software agency will pay an outside specialist to take a bounded deployment or operations workstream off its product team's plate.

  • Buyer: agency owner, CTO or engineering/delivery lead.
  • Starting project: one application's release pipeline, one repeatable deployment or one manual operational workflow.
  • Commercial model: quote after technical review. Use a separately agreed paid diagnostic when the implementation cannot yet be scoped.
  • Success: a suitable paid project and a useful understanding of sales effort, delivery hours and margin—not just page views.

The first two weeks

  1. Days 1–2 · Get ready to be credible

    Publish the offer with a working email-first inquiry route. Prepare a short, clearly labeled sandbox demonstration showing a deployment change, verification and handoff. Confirm availability, contractual clearance and how paid work will be scoped.

    Ready now: service page and email brief builder. The public 1Password guide is a technical-writing sample, not a deployment case study.

  2. Days 3–5 · Test a small outreach batch

    Review ten researched agencies. Select five based on relevant services or case studies, a public business contact route, and a specific reason for a partnership conversation. Send individually reviewed messages; ask whether outside specialists are useful rather than asserting a need.

  3. Days 6–10 · Follow up and qualify

    Follow up once after 5–7 business days, then stop. For interested teams, establish the actual problem, ownership, access permissions, target date, budget and acceptance criteria. Propose a bounded implementation or a paid diagnostic—not free open-ended debugging.

  4. Days 11–14 · Decide what to repeat

    Compare substantive replies, qualified needs, proposals, paid projects and total hours. If the fit is weak, change the audience or the offer before increasing volume. If a project works commercially, deliver it, seek permission for an honest case study, and expand to thirty similar prospects.

A clear finish line

For a first deployment project, agree on one application and environment, the expected release behavior, a recovery procedure, who approves production changes and what evidence counts as acceptance.

  • Reviewable changes in the client's repository and tooling.
  • Verification against the agreed scope.
  • Deployment and recovery instructions.
  • Explicit ownership and access handoff.
  • A bounded defect-correction window, with maintenance separately scoped.

Not included: 24/7 response, unlimited support, guaranteed uptime, unscoped migrations or compliance attestations.

Spend after evidence, not before.

The initial channels are a small researched agency batch and, separately, suitable active project listings. The landing page supports those conversations and can accumulate organic search traffic; it does not produce demand merely by existing.

Do not buy a large contact list, start paid ads, build a SaaS or sell a workflow subscription before a customer has paid for the underlying outcome. Ads require a separately approved spending cap and qualified-inquiry measurement.

Keep the experiment honest

  • Published services show relevance, not an active buying requirement.
  • Leave unknown names, technologies and contact addresses blank rather than guessing.
  • Use appropriate professional contact channels, comply with applicable outreach rules and honor opt-outs.
  • Use only permissioned material in demonstrations; no client secrets, private logs or customer data in initial inquiries.
  • No automated outreach or ad spending is part of this published plan.
